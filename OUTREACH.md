# OUTREACH — Asking creators for better footage (drafts, not sent)

## First: the strategic correction

**You do not need permission from Pexels creators.** Their license already grants worldwide, commercial, no-attribution use. Emailing them "can we use your footage?" wastes the contact and makes you look like you don't know the rules.

What they *can* give you that the license can't:

1. **The uncut original** — Pexels clips are trimmed to 6–120s. Field recordists and drone pilots usually have 5–20 minute takes.
2. **The high-bitrate master** — Pexels re-encodes to ~10 Mbps. Their camera files are 100–400 Mbps.
3. **A custom shot** — "a slow 3-minute drone push over fog in your local forest" is a weekend errand for someone who already flies there.
4. **A relationship** — the only real moat in a stock-footage channel.

So the ask is always: *longer, cleaner, or custom* — never *may I*.

## Rules before sending (non-negotiable)

- One email per person. No BCC, no template smell, no attachments.
- Name the exact clip, so they know you actually watched it.
- Offer something concrete: video credit + description link + channel credits page + pinned comment + a link from our site if we ever have one.
- Say plainly there is no payment. Never imply future payment.
- No more than ~120 words. Creators delete long mail.
- Never ask for the raw file in the first message. Ask if they'd be open to it.
- Everything goes from **superman18yyys@gmail.com** — get the user's explicit OK before any send.

## Where people actually need this material

Verified facts first, then hypotheses clearly labelled.

**Verified (licenses read in-session):**
- Pexels, Coverr, Mixkit — free commercial stock, no attribution → they *want* contributors, not buyers.
- Freesound — CC0/CC BY audio, community of recordists who explicitly want their work used.
- Wikimedia Commons — PD/CC, the safest redistribution surface.

**Hypotheses to test (not yet verified):**

1. **Stock audio marketplaces** — Pond5, AudioJungle, Artlist, Soundstripe. Rain beds sell as loopable 1–5 minute files. Our differentiator would be *long* clean takes, which is exactly what those marketplaces lack.
2. **Sleep/meditation app developers** — they need licensed ambience beds and hate per-stream royalties. One deal beats 1,000 YouTube views.
3. **Podcasters and ASMR channels** — always short of beds that don't loop obviously.
4. **Hospitality** — hotels, spas, saunas, airports buy ambient loops for in-room/install playback. Usually a one-off licence fee.
5. **Faceless/TikTok channels** — need b-roll for rain/study content; they pay per clip on Fiverr.
6. **Creator communities where a "credit for footage" trade is normal** — r/FieldRecording, r/drone, r/videography, drone Discords, and the contributor forums of Pexels/Pixabay. This is where the email approach actually works, because those people are pre-disposed to share.

## The thing to be honest about

Credit is worth very little to a creator with 500k downloads. What they want is *distribution to an audience that will click through* — so the pitch only works if the channel has real views. Until then, the honest offer is: "small channel, real credit, no money, totally fine to say no." That gets replies. Pretending otherwise gets ignored.
